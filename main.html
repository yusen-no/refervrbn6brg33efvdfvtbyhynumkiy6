Physical Mechanisms and Mathematical Models

Optical absorption and intensity attenuation are fundamentally determined by the interaction between material properties, photon energy, and absorber thickness:

1. Energy Threshold and Basic Condition

  • The band gap Eg of a semiconductor (corresponding to the maximum wavelength λmax) establishes the fundamental threshold for optical absorption.
  • For photon energies below the band gap (ℏω < Eg, or wavelengths greater than λmax), the absorption coefficient is zero (α = 0), rendering the material completely transparent.
  • Optical absorption only occurs when the photon energy meets or exceeds the band gap (ℏω ≥ Eg).

2. Absorption Coefficients and Band Structure Mechanisms

Once the energy threshold is satisfied, the growth rate of the absorption coefficient α depends heavily on the crystal's electronic band structure:

  • Direct Band Gap Materials (e.g., InGaAs): Electron transitions occur directly at the zone center where k = 0. The absorption coefficient is proportional to the square root of the energy difference:
    α(ℏω) ∝ (ℏω - Eg)1/2
  • Indirect Band Gap Materials (e.g., Silicon): The conduction band minimum occurs away from k = 0, requiring lattice vibrations (phonons) to assist with momentum conservation. The absorption coefficient is proportional to the squared energy difference:
    αindirect(ℏω) ∝ (ℏω - Eg ∓ ℏΩ)2

3. Exponential Intensity Attenuation (Beer-Lambert Law)

As photons with sufficient energy propagate through the material, light intensity experiences cumulative exponential decay with penetration depth (z). This is described by the Beer-Lambert Law:

I(z) = I0 · e-αz

Where I0 represents the incident light intensity, and α is the absorption coefficient determined by photon energy and material characteristics. This law dictates the exact fraction of light absorbed versus the remaining transmitted intensity.
